# Fastener Compliance: Understanding CBAM Data, Chromium-Free Specifications and EN 10204 Certificates

Fastener purchasing increasingly involves questions beyond size, strength and availability. Customers may request production emissions data, specify hexavalent-chromium-free coatings, or require an EN 10204 inspection document.

For suppliers and customers, the challenge is to understand what each request needs to demonstrate. An emissions report, a coating declaration and a material inspection certificate serve different purposes. Getting those requirements clear before ordering helps prevent delays, unnecessary costs and gaps in traceability.

## CBAM emissions requests: what can a fastener supplier provide?

The EU Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism, or CBAM, entered its definitive regime on 1 January 2026. It places obligations on importers of covered goods, including reporting embedded emissions and purchasing and surrendering CBAM certificates. The current rules include an annual 50-tonne mass threshold for relevant goods, assessed at importer level. [European Commission CBAM guidance](https://taxation-customs.ec.europa.eu/carbon-border-adjustment-mechanism/cbam-definitive-regime_en)

Iron and steel fasteners classified under heading 7318 are included in CBAM's product coverage. However, applicability must be checked against the goods actually imported into the EU, their classification, origin and relevant exemptions. [CBAM Regulation, Annex I](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2023/956/oj/eng)

For an Australian fastener supplier, two situations need different treatment.

**Fasteners exported separately to the EU:** the EU importer may need CBAM information for the fasteners themselves.

**Fasteners incorporated into a machine in Australia:** the finished machine's customs classification determines whether that imported product is covered. The presence of steel fasteners does not automatically make the whole machine subject to CBAM.

A machine builder may still request component emissions information for its own carbon accounting or customer requirements. Suppliers should establish whether a request concerns statutory CBAM reporting, a product carbon footprint or a broader sustainability assessment.

### Start by clarifying the request

Before approaching manufacturers for data, confirm:

- Which products, quantities and deliveries are involved?
- Is the request for actual production emissions or information supporting the use of official default values?
- Which production period and manufacturing installation must the data cover?
- What calculation method, verification and supporting evidence are required?

Actual CBAM emissions data must follow the applicable methodology and verification requirements. A generic sustainability statement or company-wide carbon footprint does not automatically satisfy those requirements. The Commission also permits the use of official default values under the definitive regime. [European Commission implementation guidance](https://customs-taxation.learning.europa.eu/pluginfile.php/70425/mod_resource/content/1/Webinar%20takeaways%20-%20CBAM%20has%20launched%20-%20What%20changes%20for%20me.pdf)

### The supplier's role is to preserve the evidence

A distributor can help connect the customer's request with the relevant manufacturer and production records. Useful information may include manufacturing installation details, production route, reporting period, emissions per tonne of product and supporting verification.

The link between the data and the supplied goods matters. The same SKU may be sourced from different factories or production routes, so one emissions figure should not automatically be applied across all stock.

Where actual data is unavailable, that limitation should be stated clearly. Suppliers should avoid presenting estimates, unrelated factory averages or general environmental declarations as verified CBAM data.

The Commission's portal for non-EU installation operators allows manufacturers to share installation and emissions information with relevant declarants. This can help manage repeated requests while controlling access to production data. [European Commission CBAM Registry](https://taxation-customs.ec.europa.eu/carbon-border-adjustment-mechanism/cbam-registry_en)

## Hexavalent-chromium-free coatings: specify more than the colour

Descriptions such as "zinc plated", "yellow zinc" or "clear zinc" do not fully define a fastener's coating chemistry or performance.

Historically, some zinc coating treatments used hexavalent chromium, also called chromium VI or Cr(VI). Alternative systems include treatments using trivalent chromium, Cr(III), as well as chromium-free options.

**Hexavalent-chromium-free does not mean entirely chromium-free.** A specification permitting trivalent chromium is different from one prohibiting chromium in every form. The wording should reflect the application's actual requirement.

Appearance is also an unreliable guide. A coating's colour alone cannot establish whether it contains Cr(VI). Fastener supplier guidance identifies Cr(VI)-free passivation systems with finishes ranging from clear and bluish to iridescent or yellowish. [Bossard corrosion protection guidance](https://www.bossard.com/global-en/-/media/bossard-group/website/documents/technical-resources/en/f-033-en.pdf)

### Define the complete coating system

A useful purchasing specification should address:

- The coating standard and applicable edition.
- Base coating, such as zinc or zinc-nickel.
- Coating thickness and dimensional requirements.
- Whether Cr(VI)-free or entirely chromium-free chemistry is required.
- Passivation, sealer, topcoat and lubrication.
- Corrosion test requirements and acceptance criteria.
- Friction requirements where controlled tightening is important.
- Required declarations or test evidence.

ISO 4042 covers electroplated fastener coating systems and includes requirements and recommendations addressing hydrogen embrittlement risk. The specified system therefore needs to suit the fastener's material, strength and intended use. [ISO 4042](https://www.iso.org/standard/77913.html)

For high-strength fasteners, a change of finish should receive engineering review. Coating processes can affect embrittlement risk, while sealers and lubricants can change tightening behaviour.

### Request evidence against the agreed specification

A coating declaration should identify the products or batches covered and the requirement being confirmed. Where testing is required, agree the method and acceptance criteria before purchase.

Cr(VI)-free status alone does not establish complete RoHS or REACH compliance. Those assessments may involve other substances and product-specific obligations.

## EN 10204: what is the difference between 2.1, 2.2 and 3.1?

EN 10204 defines types of inspection documents for metallic products. The differences concern the evidence provided, whether inspection is specific to the supplied products, and who validates the document.

| Document | What it provides | Important distinction |
|---|---|---|
| **Type 2.1 — Declaration of compliance with the order** | A manufacturer's statement that the supplied products comply with the order, without test results. | It confirms compliance but provides no numerical test evidence. |
| **Type 2.2 — Test report** | A manufacturer's statement of compliance supported by results from non-specific inspection. | The products tested are not necessarily the products supplied. |
| **Type 3.1 — Inspection certificate** | A manufacturer's statement of compliance supported by results from specific inspection. | It is validated by the manufacturer's authorised inspection representative, independent of the manufacturing department. |

These distinctions are explained in the British Stainless Steel Association's guidance on EN 10204 inspection documents. [BSSA certificate guidance](https://bssa.org.uk/bssa_articles/bsen-10204-test-certificates-for-stainless-steel-products/)

### A 3.1 certificate does not automatically mean third-party certification

The independent representative validating a 3.1 certificate can work for the manufacturer. Independence from the manufacturing department does not require an external inspection organisation.

A 3.1 certificate also does not mean every fastener has been individually tested. Sampling, test units and acceptance requirements depend on the applicable product specification and order.

### Check what the certificate actually covers

A raw-material certificate may establish the chemistry of the steel used to manufacture a bolt. It does not, by itself, establish the finished bolt's mechanical properties, dimensional conformity or coating performance.

Customers should check that the documentation covers the properties they need to verify and that its references can be linked to the supplied goods.

Similarly, a distributor's general certificate of conformity should not be assumed to be an EN 10204 type 3.1 certificate.

### Specify certification before ordering

Certificate availability depends on the original manufacturing, inspection and traceability arrangements. Existing stock may not have the records needed to support a later request for specific inspection documentation.

Adding a "3.1" heading to a general report cannot create the missing evidence. Where specific certification is needed, include it in the purchase order and confirm availability before accepting substitutes.

## Match each request to the evidence needed

A practical purchasing brief should distinguish three questions:

- **CBAM:** What production emissions information is required, for which goods and reporting purpose?
- **Coatings:** What chemistry, corrosion performance and assembly characteristics must the finish achieve?
- **Inspection documents:** What conformity and test evidence must accompany the supplied fasteners?

Keeping these requirements clear helps suppliers source the appropriate products and helps customers assess the evidence they receive.

For projects involving EU exports, controlled coatings or specific inspection requirements, discuss the application and documentation with United Fasteners before ordering so availability and supporting records can be confirmed.
