# Fastener Compliance in Australia and New Zealand: QA/QC Documents and EN 10204 Explained

*What manufacturers, importers, distributors and buyers need to know about test certificates, verification testing and traceability.*

A shipment of structural bolts arrives with an EN 10204 type 3.1 certificate. The certificate lists test results, identifies a batch and carries an authorised signature.

Does that make the shipment compliant with Australian and New Zealand requirements?

**The certificate may support compliance, but its title alone does not establish it.** Buyers must check the applicable product standard, the required conformity assessment and whether the documents can be traced to the goods received.

For fastener suppliers, compliance starts with defining those requirements before placing the order.

## EN 10204 and AS/NZS standards serve different purposes

EN 10204 defines types of inspection documents for metallic products. It distinguishes between declarations without test results, reports based on non-specific inspection and certificates based on specific inspection. It also defines who validates each document. [BSI](https://knowledge.bsigroup.com/products/metallic-products-types-of-inspection-documents)

| EN 10204 type | Evidence provided | Validation |
|---|---|---|
| **2.1** | Declaration of compliance with the order, without test results | Manufacturer |
| **2.2** | Compliance statement and non-specific inspection results; tested goods are not necessarily those supplied | Manufacturer |
| **3.1** | Compliance statement and specific inspection results | Manufacturer's authorised inspection representative, independent of the manufacturing department |
| **3.2** | Compliance statement and specific inspection results, with additional validation | Manufacturer's independent inspection representative together with the purchaser's authorised representative or an inspector designated by regulations |

A type 3.1 certificate does not inherently require an external inspection body. Type 3.2 involves the additional representative specified by the standard. [EN 10204, Annex A](https://kalibresteel.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/EN-10204.pdf)

Australian and New Zealand product standards address a different question: **what must the product meet, and how must conformity be demonstrated?**

The documentation standard and product standard therefore work together. An EN 10204 certificate does not replace the technical requirements of an AS/NZS fastener standard.

## First identify which fastener standard applies

There is no single documentation package suitable for every fastener supplied in Australia and New Zealand.

General-purpose bolts, high-strength structural assemblies, stainless steel fasteners and foundation rods can have different specifications and acceptance requirements. The purchase order should identify the product standard, edition, grade or property class, dimensions, finish and required evidence.

For high-strength structural bolt assemblies, two particularly relevant standards are:

| Standard | Main function |
|---|---|
| **AS/NZS 1252.1:2016** | Technical and conformity requirements for high-strength structural bolt, nut and washer assemblies |
| **AS/NZS 1252.2:2016** | Additional verification testing for bolt assemblies |

The Australian Steel Institute explains that Part 2 must be separately called up by the purchaser or specifier; specifying Part 1 alone does not automatically invoke it. ASI recommends specifying assemblies to Part 1 with verification to Part 2. [ASI Technical Note TN016](https://www.steel.org.au/Membership/media/Australian-Steel-Institute/PDFs/Tech-Note-TN016-Installation-of-bolted-connections-V1.pdf)

These requirements should not be extended indiscriminately to all fasteners.

The applicable edition also matters. In New Zealand's Building Code system, cited standards have specified versions and may have modifications. A newer publication should not automatically be substituted for the edition required by the project's compliance pathway. [MBIE](https://www.building.govt.nz/building-code-compliance/how-the-building-code-works/standards/introduction)

## What documents should accompany structural fastener supply?

For assemblies supplied to AS/NZS 1252.1 and verified to AS/NZS 1252.2, buyers should distinguish manufacturing evidence from local-market verification.

| Evidence | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Manufacturing conformity documentation | Establishes the product's manufacturing and assessment basis |
| Lot-specific inspection and test reports | Records results applicable to the supplied manufacturing lots |
| Verification test reports | Demonstrates the additional assessment required under Part 2 |
| Supplier Declaration of Conformity—SDoC | Formally declares conformity for the identified product or batches |
| Packaging and traceability records | Connects the supplied assemblies to their supporting evidence |

ASI's guidance describes manufacturing assessment evidence, lot results and the SDoC supporting Part 2 conformity. Its recommended document review includes product identity, batch references, test results and authorised validation. An SDoC should identify its issuer, the product, applicable requirements and the responsible signatory. [ASI Technical Note TN001](https://www.steel.org.au/getattachment/a1825eac-9244-4405-b68b-9f9616fdd2f6/Tech-Note-TN001-v5_web.pdf)

**A generic supplier CoC should not be assumed to fulfil the SDoC requirements.** Its content and supporting evidence need to be checked.

Likewise, where Part 2 verification is required, a manufacturer's EN 10204 type 3.1 certificate alone does not demonstrate that this additional verification has been completed.

## Check what was tested—not just the certificate heading

Consider a finished, coated structural bolt assembly supported only by a steel mill certificate.

That certificate may establish the chemistry of the original steel heat. It does not, by itself, establish the finished bolt's strength, the nut's proof-load performance, the washer's hardness or the assembly's required functional performance.

As a practical document-review step, separate evidence into:

- **Material:** original steel or other feedstock.
- **Finished components:** bolts, nuts and washers.
- **Processing:** heat treatment and coating, where relevant.
- **Assembly:** required functional and verification testing.

The applicable standards and order determine the tests and sampling. A supplier's preferred certificate format should not determine the acceptance criteria.

## Laboratory accreditation must cover the tests

Where accredited testing is required, checking the laboratory's name or logo is only the first step.

NATA explains that accreditation applies to a defined scope. Buyers should confirm that the relevant tests fall within that scope and identify any results outside the accreditation endorsement. [NATA test reports explained](https://nata.com.au/accreditation/nata-test-reports-explained/)

For example, evidence of accreditation for chemical analysis does not automatically establish accredited capability for fastener mechanical or assembly testing.

Also distinguish **laboratory accreditation** from **EN 10204 document validation**. They answer different questions: competence to perform a test and responsibility for validating the inspection document.

## A practical compliance workflow for the supply chain

The following workflow is recommended purchasing and quality-management practice. It complements the applicable standards rather than replacing their detailed requirements.

### 1. Define the evidence before ordering

Record the exact standards and editions, product designation, finish, inspection-document type, verification requirements and customer-specific conditions.

Assign responsibility for arranging tests, reviewing results and issuing declarations. Agree when the documents must be provided.

### 2. Review the supplier's capability

Confirm who manufactures the goods and who performs outsourced processing. Review whether the proposed evidence covers the actual products, sizes and production arrangements.

A quality-system certificate can support supplier assessment, but it does not demonstrate conformity of an individual shipment.

### 3. Approve the documents before release

Check product descriptions, lot references, results, acceptance limits, laboratory details and signatures.

Resolve missing or inconsistent evidence before accepting the goods. A declaration does not override a failed result.

### 4. Preserve traceability during receiving and distribution

Keep manufacturing lots identifiable through storage, repacking and split deliveries. Link internal stock references to original supplier documents.

Where an assembly's conformity evidence depends on identified components, preserve that association. Substituting a component needs review against the applicable requirements.

### 5. Control changes and retain the evidence

Assess changes to manufacturing source, heat treatment, coating or assembly composition before continuing supply.

Keep the approved order, original reports, declarations, receipt checks and dispatch references together. Set retention periods against the applicable standards, contracts and project requirements rather than assuming one period fits every product.

## Where ISO 16228 fits

ISO 16228 is specifically written for finished-fastener inspection documents. It covers declarations and test reports designated **F2.1, F2.2, F3.1 and F3.2**, issued by manufacturers, distributors or relevant authorised representatives.

It can help buyers specify clearer documentation at the time of ordering. Its document types should be expressly agreed rather than treated as automatically interchangeable with EN 10204 types. [ISO 16228](https://www.iso.org/standard/55935.html)

## The acceptance question

When reviewing a fastener shipment, ask:

**Do these documents demonstrate conformity of these goods to every applicable requirement?**

That means checking the product standard, document scope, required verification, actual results and traceability together.

A well-presented certificate is useful evidence. A compliant supply chain connects that evidence to the correct products, the required assessment and the goods delivered.
