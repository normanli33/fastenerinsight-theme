# EU Product Identifiers, CBAM and Anti-Dumping: What Actually Applies to Fasteners Bound for Europe

From 1 November 2026, EU customs requires Product Identifiers (PIDs) on imported distance sales. Carrier notices about it have landed in many procurement inboxes, and some AI assistants are telling fastener distributors to rebuild their item masters around it.

**That advice mixes up three separate regimes.** PID is a parcel-data rule. For industrial fasteners, CBAM, anti-dumping duty and the new Machinery Regulation matter far more.

## What the PID rule actually covers

PID is part of the EU's low-value e-commerce reform. Each declaration line needs:

- **M-PID** — the seller's own SKU (mandatory)
- **NS-PID** — the manufacturer's own part number (mandatory)
- **S-PID** — a GTIN/EAN/UPC, where one exists

It applies to goods **sold at a distance to EU consumers** and imported from outside the EU. A machine shipped B2B to a German plant is not a distance sale. The bolts inside it are not declared line by line. **No PID obligation travels down to the component supplier.**

A common data error: `DIN933-M10X50-A4` is a standard designation, not a manufacturer part number. DIN 933 also now sits under ISO 4017. An NS-PID must be the factory's own code.

## The three regimes compared

| Regime | Triggered by | Hits fasteners inside a machine? | Hits loose fasteners (spares kits) into EU? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Product Identifier (PID) | B2C distance sale into the EU | No | Only if sold to consumers online |
| CBAM | Import of Annex I goods (CN 7318 is listed) | Not yet — downstream extension proposed for 2028 | Yes, above 50 t per importer per year |
| EU anti-dumping (Reg. 2022/191) | Chinese-origin carbon-steel fasteners | No | Yes — stainless is excluded |
| Machinery Regulation 2023/1230 | Machine placed on EU market from 20 Jan 2027 | Indirectly, via the builder's technical file | No |

## CBAM: already live for fasteners

Screws, bolts, nuts and washers of iron or steel (CN 7318) are already CBAM goods. The definitive phase started on 1 January 2026. The EU importer reports embedded emissions and buys certificates once it passes 50 tonnes a year.

Trilogue is now underway on extending CBAM to downstream products, including machinery parts, with a 2028 target. If that passes, the steel inside a machine — fasteners included — may need emissions data. **This is the request your machine-builder customers are most likely to send you next.**

## Anti-dumping: here, origin does matter

Chinese-origin carbon-steel fasteners imported loose into the EU carry duties of 22.1–86.5 percent. The current measure expires on 18 February 2027 unless an expiry review is opened. Watch for that notice. Fasteners built into a machine are not caught, but spares shipped separately are.

## What to drop from the checklist

- **GPSR** covers consumer products, not industrial components.
- **RoHS** matters only when the finished product is electrical equipment in RoHS scope.
- **Coatings:** older yellow-chromate zinc and early zinc-flake systems used hexavalent chromium. Specify Cr(VI)-free and get it in writing.

## Takeaways

**Buyers:** Get mill-level emissions data from steel fastener suppliers now. Record country of origin and carbon vs stainless on every SKU.

**Engineers:** The Machinery Regulation drives your customers' documentation. Expect requests for property class, standard and coating per assembly, not PIDs.

**Quality & documentation:** Hold an EN 10204 3.1 certificate, REACH Article 33 statement and Cr(VI)-free coating declaration per range.

## Bottom Line

PID is a parcel rule; it won't touch a fastener distributor selling B2B. CBAM and anti-dumping already do, for any fasteners crossing the EU border on their own. If your customers export machines, prepare emissions data, origin and material records — not GTINs.
