A fastener marking tells you where to start. An ISO 8.8 bolt, an SAE Grade 5 bolt and an ASTM F3125 Grade A325 structural bolt can fall in a similar tensile-strength range, yet each belongs to a different specification. The tables below identify the governing standard and show what each designation tells you before you order, inspect or approve a fastener.

Reduced loadability: what the leading zero means

8.8 and 08.8. ISO 898-1 uses 08.8 for a reduced-loadability fastener with the material properties of class 8.8. Its finished-product load depends on the limiting geometry and the applicable requirements. A leading zero gives no universal reduction factor. ISO 898-1:2013

A4-80 and A4-080. Under ISO 3506-1, 080 is the reduced-loadability marking symbol for class 80 when the applicable threaded-section tensile test can be performed. The class-level tensile value alone does not establish the load the complete screw can carry through its head or shank. For reduced-loadability fasteners whose nominal threaded length b is below 3d and prevents that test, ISO 3506-1 calls for the stainless grade marking and omits the property-class designation. See clause 10.1.3 and Table 14. ISO 3506-1:2020

Identify the product standard before classifying the screw. ISO 10642 hexagon socket countersunk screws and ISO 7380-1 hexagon socket button-head screws have reduced loadability by design. A conventional ISO 4762 socket head cap screw belongs to a different product standard. The presence of a socket or the inability to grip a short screw in a tensile fixture is insufficient to classify it as reduced-loadability. ISO 10642:2026 · ISO 7380-1:2022 · ISO 4762:2004

What to specify and ask the lab to verify

  1. Order the complete fastener. Record the product standard and edition, mechanical-property standard and designation, thread size and pitch, length, finish and any required nut or washer. For example, an M24 × 3 × 200 mm class 8.8 bolt also needs a product standard and finish.
  2. Check test applicability for the actual geometry. Ask the lab which finished-fastener tensile, wedge or proof-load tests apply to the steel part, or which ISO 3506-1 tests apply to the stainless part. For short parts, use the applicable method and acceptance criteria specified for the size, class and geometry. A specified torsional breaking test, where applicable, is a controlled mechanical test. A torque reading from tightening a nut measures joint behaviour and cannot establish tensile compliance. ISO 898-1:2013 · ISO 3506-1:2020 · ISO 16047
  3. Match the report to the delivered lot. Verify traceability, standard and edition, actual sample size and geometry, method, measured result, units, acceptance limit and disposition. For a reduced-loadability design, ask for the applicable finished-product breaking load and the basis for it. A mill certificate describes the material and cannot by itself establish the mechanical performance of the finished geometry.
